\begin{thmbox}
\textbf{Lemma 1.} Let $p$ be a prime number. If $p$ can be represented in the form
\[
p = x^2 - xy + y^2
\]
for some integers $x,y$, then either $p=3$ or $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$.
\end{thmbox}

\begin{proof}
Modulo $3$ we have $-xy\equiv 2xy$, hence
\[
x^2 - xy + y^2 \equiv x^2+2xy+y^2=(x+y)^2 \pmod 3.
\]
Every square is congruent to $0$ or $1$ modulo $3$, so $x^2-xy+y^2\equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 3$ for all integers $x,y$, and never $2 \pmod 3$.

Now suppose that a prime $p$ satisfies $p = x^2 - xy + y^2$. Then $p\equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 3$. If $p \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$, then $p=3$ since $p$ is prime. Otherwise $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$.
\end{proof}
